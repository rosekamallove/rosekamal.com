## The Problem

Cold outreach at scale generates a lot of replies. Most of them follow predictable patterns — interested, not interested, wrong person, out of office, ask for more info. Handling them manually is repetitive work that slows down the top of the funnel.

The goal: build a system that reads every reply, understands what the person actually means, and sends a good response — without a human touching it.

## Architecture

The system runs as a background job pipeline:

1. **Inbound webhook** — a Resend inbound email webhook fires on every reply, pushing the raw email into a BullMQ queue.
2. **Classifier** — a Claude call reads the reply and outputs one of a fixed intent set: `interested`, `not_interested`, `wrong_person`, `out_of_office`, `needs_info`, `unsubscribe`.
3. **Draft agent** — a second Claude call takes the classified intent and the original email thread as context, and drafts a reply. The prompt includes the sender's role, company, and any notes from the CRM.
4. **Peer reviewer** — a third Claude call reviews the draft for tone, accuracy, and whether it actually addresses what was asked. It either approves or returns edits.
5. **Sender** — approved drafts go out via Resend. Rejections loop back once, then escalate to a human-review queue if they fail again.

// Simplified intent classification
const result = await claude.messages.create({
  model: "claude-opus-4-5",
  max_tokens: 256,
  system: CLASSIFIER_SYSTEM_PROMPT,
  messages: [
    {
      role: "user",
      content: `Classify this reply:\n\n${emailBody}`,
    },

## What Works Well

- **Intent classification** is very reliable — the fixed label set keeps the model from hallucinating novel categories.
- **The peer-review step** catches most of the bad drafts before they go out. It's cheap (short context, low token count) and catches obvious problems.
- **BullMQ** gives you retries, dead-letter queues, and concurrency limits out of the box. Running this without a proper queue was a mistake I made early on.

## What's Still Rough

- Replies that quote long previous threads inflate the context and can confuse the classifier. Still experimenting with stripping quoted text before classification.
- The peer reviewer sometimes over-edits — produces a draft that's technically correct but sounds generic. Working on tighter reviewer prompts.
